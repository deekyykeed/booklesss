# Agent Brief — Write a Piece That Convinces a Student to Sign Up to Booklesss

## Your one job
Write a short, persuasive piece aimed at one university student. It has a single
purpose: make that student want Booklesss, and get them to take the action at the end —
go to Google, search **Booklesss** (three S's), and sign up. Every sentence must move the
reader closer to that action. If a line doesn't push toward the sign-up, cut it.
Conversion is the only measure of success. Do not write an "explainer" that informs and
stops. Write something that makes the reader act before they close the tab.

## Who is reading it
A Zambian university student (business and finance courses first). Right now they study
from old, secondhand, photocopied notes that don't match what their lecturer actually
teaches. They work hard, but the material is against them. They've seen a hundred
"study group" pitches and they are tired of hype. They will only act if it feels true and
built for them, not sold to them.

## Write it TO them, not about them
- Second person ("you"). Talk to one student, the night before an exam, like a friend who
  has been exactly where they are.
- Peer-to-peer, honest, plain. No corporate voice, no chatbot voice, no advert voice.
- Persuade with truth and recognition, not superlatives. Booklesss never oversells.

## The persuasion arc (keep this order)
1. **Name the pain so they feel seen.** Open on the exact experience of revising from bad,
   borrowed, outdated notes with nobody to ask at 11pm. Be specific enough that they think
   "this is literally me."
2. **Take the shame off them.** They're not behind because they're lazy or not smart enough.
   They're behind because nobody ever built them good material. Reframe the blame from the
   student onto the material.
3. **Show the way out — what Booklesss is.** Clean, current notes for their exact course this
   semester; a community they're inside, not a folder they forget; small steps they actually
   finish; real people who answer when they're stuck.
4. **Close the escape routes.** Everything else they've tried — last year's notes with a new
   date, the dead group chat, a random class-WhatsApp PDF, a YouTube video for another
   country's syllabus — is someone else's material. Booklesss is the only thing made for them,
   here, now. Say plainly that nothing else is doing this.
5. **Make them picture the after.** Understanding their notes on the first read; walking into
   the exam having actually followed the material all term; never studying alone again.
6. **Ask for the action, once and clearly.** No hedging, no soft "maybe check it out."

## The ending action (required — this is the whole point)
Everything builds to the sign-up. The close must:
- Tell them exactly what to do: if there's a link, tap it; if not, **open Google and search
  Booklesss — that's Booklesss with three S's, B-O-O-K-L-E-S-S-S. We'll be the first thing
  you see.**
- Make signing up feel easy, obvious, and low-risk — the natural next move, not a big leap.
- End on a line that pushes them to do it now.

## Lower the risk of acting (use the real offer, don't invent one)
A student signs up faster when the downside is small. If Booklesss currently has a
risk-reversal — a free first month, a money-back guarantee, or similar — name it near the
close so signing up feels safe. **Do not invent an offer, price, or deadline.** Pull the
current one from `Operations/pricing-strategy.md`, and if you can't confirm it, leave the
offer out rather than guess. (The old "founding rate" deadline has passed — never use it.)

## Voice & hard rules
- Plain English, short sentences, no jargon.
- Banned words: tapestry, nuance, multifaceted, robust, delve, foster, Furthermore,
  It's worth noting, landscape, journey, empower, leverage (as a verb), game-changer,
  seamless, holistic, synergy.
- Any example uses Zambian companies (Zanaco, Zambeef, ZESCO, First Quantum) and ZMW.
  No fake round numbers.
- One em dash maximum. No forced lists of three. No emoji.
- Don't promise features that don't exist. What is true and safe to claim: notes rebuilt
  per course, a community (hosted largely on Slack), steps with visible progress, real
  people to ask, material kept current.

## What "good" looks like
The student reads it in under two minutes, feels it was written about their exact
situation, believes Booklesss is the only real fix, and does the search / signs up before
moving on. It should feel like a true story about their week that happens to end with the
one thing that fixes it. Make them act.
